This preprint examines how coordinated AI agents could support disinformation campaigns in African contexts, with a focus on Mali and Ethiopia. It considers how political institutions, media conditions, language coverage, and human involvement shape both potential threats and defensive capacity. The analysis distinguishes malicious AI swarms from simpler uses of AI to generate content or manage synthetic accounts.

The paper presents a forward-looking risk analysis, not evidence that fully autonomous influence swarms already operate at scale in Africa. It proposes safeguards combining technical measures, platform accountability, stronger civic institutions, and regional cooperation.
